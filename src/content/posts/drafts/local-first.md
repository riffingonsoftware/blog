You almost certainly carry around more compute than was used to put humanity on the moon. It's not a fair comparison, but it's still useful to consider.

Listen, most things come in cycles, or perhaps the often misattributed, "history doesn't repeat itself, but it often rhymes." I get that, and perhaps you, weary reader, may stumble upon this post at some future point wherein we largely run local software, but that's not the world in which I write this. I live in a time where documents, who truly wondrously allow for live, online collaboration, must be explicitly prepared for offline editing, and even then your work may be blown away if the server decides too much change has happened in the meantime. Listen, I get it, that's a stupid hard problem. (See [CRDTs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict-free_replicated_data_type) if you're interested.) So, let's just consider that a maybe bad example to illustrate how _not_ using local-first can be a hassle, focusing on the pre-work required to work offline. I would hazard a guess many documents have a solo editor, and if I forgot to prep for some offline editing before hopping on many forms of transportation, well, I'm out of luck.
