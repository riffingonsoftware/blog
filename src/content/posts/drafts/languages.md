So... I've [previously](../2023/20230303) mentioned Andrej Karpathy's comment about [English being the way ahead](https://twitter.com/karpathy/status/1617979122625712128). Obviously, it's bounced around my skull since. I'm increasingly convinced he is correct.

I used to be a HUGE believer in [Dave Farley](https://www.youtube.com/c/ContinuousDelivery). Anymore, I've become less enamored (it's me, not him), but he has made an interesting point about English as a programming language. I believe I first heard him discuss this before [this video](https://youtu.be/MUol_5xvFTI), but it's the first I found looking just now. To very much paraphrase his point (I recommend you watch the video), he explains programming languages remove ambiguity present in natural language, like English. He's right, but I also think it doesn't really matter.

Ambiguity has always been part of software development. We try to avoid it. We work towards tighter feedback loops, we demo to stakeholders, we fuzz test, and we still often fail. We prototype, refactor, iterate, and still miss a ton. It feels like sometimes some developers must not even care. I'm sure you've used a piece of software and been absolutely convinced there was no way any of the developers actually used the product, it's just too busted to believe someone who could fix it had ever tried it. I know I have that experience far too often. (Ever play Madden?)

The sad truth is it doesn't matter. It never has.

Many PMs can't, or no longer do, code, except by... speaking a natural language to a bunch of agents, ... er developers. Put in natural language, get software back. If they can do it, so can you.
